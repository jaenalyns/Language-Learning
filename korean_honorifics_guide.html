Korean Honorifics Guide

Master Respectful Communication in Korean

높임말 - The Foundation of Korean Social Interaction

🏆 The Golden Rule

🎯 When in doubt, use honorifics!

It's always better to be too polite than accidentally rude. Most Koreans appreciate your effort to show respect, even if your usage isn't perfect.

📊 Step 1: Master the Three Speech Levels

🔴 Formal (하십시오체)

Endings: -습니다, -ㅂ니다, -십니까

Use with: Strangers, elderly people, very formal situations

안녕하십니까?
annyeonghasimnikka?
"How do you do?" (very formal)

🟡 Polite (해요체) ← Start Here!

Endings: -어요/-아요, -세요

Use with: Most daily interactions, colleagues, acquaintances

안녕하세요?
annyeonghaseyo?
"Hello" (polite - most common)

🟢 Casual (해체)

Endings: -어/-아, -지, -야

Use with: Close friends same age, family, younger people

안녕?
annyeong?
"Hi" (casual - careful!)

💡 Beginner Strategy

Start with Polite Level (해요체) for everyone. It's safe in 90% of situations!

🎯 What Speech Levels Affect vs. What They Don't

✅ Speech Levels DO Affect:

1. Verbs (Action Words)

  • Formal: 갑니다 (gamnida) - "go"
  • Polite: 가요 (gayo) - "go"
  • Casual: 가 (ga) - "go"

2. Adjectives (Descriptive Words)

  • Formal: 좋습니다 (jotseumnida) - "good"
  • Polite: 좋아요 (joayo) - "good"
  • Casual: 좋아 (joa) - "good"

3. Copula (Being/Existence)

  • Formal: 학생입니다 (haksaeng-imnida)
  • Polite: 학생이에요 (haksaeng-ieyo)
  • Casual: 학생이야 (haksaeng-iya)

🚫 Speech Levels DON'T Affect:

1. Titles and Honorific Suffixes

  • 선생님 (seonsaengnim) - Teacher
  • 사장님 (sajangnim) - Boss
  • -님 (-nim) - Honorific suffix

2. Pure Nouns

  • 책 (chaek) - book
  • 집 (jip) - house
  • 물 (mul) - water

3. Particles & Numbers

  • 이/가 (i/ga) - subject markers
  • 하나, 둘, 셋 (numbers)
  • 오늘, 어제 (time words)

📋 Complete Sentence Examples:

Formal: 저는 한국 사람입니다. 서울에서 왔습니다.
"I am Korean. I came from Seoul." (formal)

Polite: 저는 한국 사람이에요. 서울에서 왔어요.
"I am Korean. I came from Seoul." (polite)

Casual: 나는 한국 사람이야. 서울에서 왔어.
"I am Korean. I came from Seoul." (casual)

💡 Key Rule:

Speech levels create consistency throughout the entire conversation. You don't mix formal verb endings with casual adjective endings - everything should match the same level of politeness!

Titles like 선생님 stay 선생님 whether you're speaking formally, politely, or casually. The speech level shows up in how you conjugate the verbs and adjectives around those titles!

💫 Special Case: "Want" Verb (싶다) - All Levels & Tenses

The verb "to want" uses the -고 싶다 pattern and can be conjugated to all speech levels:

Tense Formal (하십시오체) Polite (해요체) Casual (해체)
Present
"I want to eat"		 먹고 싶습니다
meok-go sip-seubnida		 먹고 싶어요
meok-go si-peo-yo		 먹고 싶어
meok-go si-peo
Past
"I wanted to eat"		 먹고 싶었습니다
meok-go sip-eot-seubnida		 먹고 싶었어요
meok-go sip-eoss-eo-yo		 먹고 싶었어
meok-go sip-eoss-eo
Future
"I will want to eat"		 먹고 싶겠습니다
meok-go sip-get-seubnida		 먹고 싶을 거예요
meok-go sip-eul geo-ye-yo		 먹고 싶을 거야
meok-go sip-eul geo-ya

🔗 Why -고 Pattern?

먹고 싶어요 = "I eat-and want" (Korean logic)

NOT 먹어요 싶어요 = "I eat, I want" (two separate sentences - wrong!)

Korean uses -고 to connect the first verb to the main verb, like English "to" in "want to eat"

🙇‍♂️ Humble Forms (겸양법)

Humble forms lower yourself to show respect to the listener. This is the other side of the honorific system:

🗣️ Humble Pronouns

Regular Form Humble Form English When to Use
나 (na) 저 (jeo) I/me Formal situations, speaking to superiors
우리 (uri) 저희 (jeohui) we/us Formal situations, representing a group

🎯 Humble Verbs

Regular Verb Humble Form Usage Context Example
주다 (juda) 드리다 (deurida) When giving to someone of higher status 선물을 드려요 "I give a gift"

🏠 Humble Nouns

Regular Noun Humble/Honorific Form English Example Usage
이름 (ireum) 성함 (seongham) name 성함이 어떻게 되세요? "What is your name?"
나이 (nai) 연세 (yeonse) age 연세가 어떻게 되세요? "How old are you?"
집 (jip) 댁 (daek) house 댁이 어디세요? "Where is your house?"

🏷️ Step 4: Basic Honorific Titles and Suffixes

Suffix Korean Respect Level Who to Use With Example
씨 (ssi) 씨 Equal/slightly respectful Colleagues, acquaintances of similar status 김영수 씨 (Kim Yeongsu-ssi)
님 (nim) 님 High respect Teachers, bosses, customers, professionals 선생님 (seonsaeng-nim)
군 (gun) 군 Formal (young men) Young unmarried males in formal contexts 지호 군 (Jiho-gun)
양 (yang) 양 Formal (young women) Young unmarried females in formal contexts 수아 양 (Sua-yang)

🔄 How Titles Integrate with Speech Levels

🟢 Casual Level

영수야, 뭐 해?

Yeongsu-ya, mwo hae?

"Yeongsu, what are you doing?"

🟡 Polite Level

영수 씨, 뭐 하세요?

Yeongsu-ssi, mwo haseyo?

"Yeongsu, what are you doing?"

🔴 Formal Level

영수 님, 무엇을 하십니까?

Yeongsu-nim, mueoseul hasimnikka?

"Yeongsu, what are you doing?"

🚫 Critical Usage Rules

  • Never use surname only with 씨: "박 씨" is extremely rude - always use full name "박영수 씨" or first name "영수 씨"
  • Professional titles require 님: Say "사장님" not "사장" - bare titles sound dismissive
  • Match title to speech level: Formal titles need formal speech, casual relationships allow casual speech

📢 Step 5: Calling Someone's Name

Korean has special endings when calling someone directly:

Ending When to Use Example Relationship Level
-아 (-a) Name ends in consonant 진영아! (Jinyeong-a!) Close friends, equal age or younger only
-야 (-ya) Name ends in vowel 예지야! (Yeji-ya!) Close friends, equal age or younger only

⚠️ Critical Rule for -아/-야

Only use with people same age or younger! Using with older people is extremely rude.

✅ Safe to Use -아/-야 With:

  • Best friends of same age
  • Younger siblings or cousins
  • Children (if you're an adult)
  • Close romantic partners

🚫 Never Use -아/-야 With:

  • Anyone older than you
  • Strangers or acquaintances
  • Teachers, bosses, or superiors
  • People you've just met

🚫 Step 6: Avoid "You" Pronouns

Korean avoids direct pronouns like "you" - use alternatives instead:

❌ Don't Say

당신은 누구예요?
dangsin-eun nugu-yeyo?
"Who are you?" (using 당신)

✅ Say Instead

성함이 어떻게 되세요?
seongham-i eotteoke doeseyo?
"What is your name?" (respectful)

🔄 Alternatives to "You"

🏷️ Use Names + Titles

김선생님은 어디 가세요?
gim-seonsaengnim-eun eodi gaseyo?
"Where are you going, Teacher Kim?"

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Use Family Terms

어머님께서 언제 오세요?
eomeonim-kkeseo eonje oseyo?
"When are you coming, Mother?"

🎯 Step 7: Honorific Particles

Replace regular particles with honorific ones for respected people:

Function Regular Particle Honorific Particle Example
Subject 이/가 (i/ga) 께서 (kkeseo) 선생님께서 오셨습니다
seonsaengnim-kkeseo osyeotseumnida
"The teacher came"
To/For 에게 (ege) 께 (kke) 어머님께 드려요
eomeonim-kke deuryeoyo
"I give to mother"

🎯 Quick Decision Guide: Who Gets What Level?

🔴 Always Use Formal/Honorific With:

  • People older than you (even by one year)
  • Teachers, professors, doctors
  • Your boss or superiors at work
  • Parents, grandparents, in-laws
  • Strangers you're meeting for the first time
  • Anyone in a position of authority

🟡 Use Polite Level With:

  • Colleagues of similar age and position
  • Acquaintances and neighbors
  • Service workers (waiters, shop clerks)
  • Classmates you don't know well
  • Most daily interactions

🟢 Can Use Casual With:

  • Close friends of the same age
  • Younger siblings and cousins
  • Children (if you're an adult)
  • Very close family members
  • People who explicitly ask you to speak casually

⚠️ Most Critical Mistakes to Avoid

❌ Wrong

저의 어머님
jeo-ui eomeonim
Using honorific for your own mother

✅ Correct

저의 어머니
jeo-ui eomeoni
Humble form for your own mother

🚨 Critical Don'ts

  • Never use honorifics for yourself when speaking to someone of higher status
  • Don't use casual speech with elders - shows deep disrespect
  • Don't mix speech levels randomly - be consistent
  • Avoid direct "you" pronouns with strangers or superiors
  • Don't call someone by name without appropriate suffix

🎓 3-Stage Learning Strategy

🌱 Stage 1: Foundation (Start Here!)

  • Master polite endings (해요체) - use this as your default
  • Learn basic titles (님, 씨)
  • Use 저 instead of 나 in formal contexts
  • Memorize family terms by gender
  • Practice consistent formality levels

🌿 Stage 2: Grammar Integration

  • Honorific verb suffixes (-시/-으시)
  • Basic humble forms (드리다, 저희)
  • Honorific particles (께서, 께)
  • Suppletive honorific verbs
  • Formal speech levels (하십시오체)

🌳 Stage 3: Advanced Mastery

  • Relative honorifics (압존법)
  • Complex social situations
  • Cultural nuances and exceptions
  • Natural code-switching between levels
  • Regional variations

💡 Effective Practice Methods

📺 Media Immersion

Watch Korean dramas, variety shows, and news. Pay attention to who uses what honorific level with whom. Family dramas are especially good for learning relationship dynamics.

🎭 Role-Playing Practice

Practice different scenarios: job interviews, meeting elders, talking to friends. Switch roles to understand different perspectives and appropriate responses.

📝 Writing Exercises

Write emails, texts, and formal letters using different honorific levels. Practice switching between casual and formal writing styles appropriately.

🗣️ Conversation Practice

Find Korean conversation partners and explicitly discuss honorific usage. Most Koreans are happy to explain appropriate levels for different situations.

🌟 Key Success Tips

  • Start conservatively: Begin with polite forms and adjust based on feedback
  • Observe and mirror: Pay attention to how Koreans speak to each other in similar situations
  • Ask when uncertain: "어떻게 말씀드릴까요?" (How should I address you?)
  • Be patient with yourself: Even native speakers sometimes struggle with complex honorific situations
  • Focus on respect: The goal is showing respect, not perfect grammar

🎯 Remember: Cultural Understanding > Perfect Grammar

Korean honorifics aren't just grammar rules - they're the key to respectful, appropriate communication in Korean society. Using them correctly shows cultural understanding and respect for Korean values. Koreans appreciate your effort to show respect, even if your usage isn't perfect!
