Understanding "요" (Yo)

"요" (yo) is a politeness marker that makes sentences formal and respectful. Here's what you need to know:

What it means:

  • "요" doesn't change the meaning of the sentence - it only adds politeness
  • It's equivalent to speaking formally or respectfully in English
  • Shows respect to the listener

When to use it:

  • With strangers - Always use 요 when speaking to people you don't know well
  • With elders - Essential when speaking to older people
  • In formal situations - Business meetings, presentations, formal events
  • With authority figures - Teachers, bosses, officials

When you can omit it:

  • With close friends - Casual speech is acceptable
  • With family - Depends on family dynamics and age relationships
  • With younger people - When you're significantly older

Understanding "저" (Jeo)

"저" (jeo) is the formal/polite version of "나" (na), both meaning "I". Here's what you need to know:

What it means:

  • "저" means "I" but in a humble, respectful way
  • It shows humility and respect when referring to yourself
  • Same meaning as "나" but with added politeness

When to use it:

  • With strangers - Always use 저 when speaking to people you don't know well
  • With elders - Essential when speaking to older people
  • In formal situations - Job interviews, business meetings, formal presentations
  • With authority figures - Teachers, bosses, government officials
  • First meetings - When introducing yourself to new people

When you can use "나" instead:

  • With close friends - Casual "나" is perfectly acceptable
  • With family - Usually "나" unless speaking to much older relatives
  • With younger people - When you're significantly older
  • Informal settings - Casual conversations with peers

Examples:

  • Formal: 저는 학생입니다 (jeoneun haksaeng-imnida) - "I am a student" (polite)
  • Informal: 나는 학생이야 (naneun haksaeng-iya) - "I am a student" (casual)

Verb Stems and Conjugation

Korean verbs change form based on politeness level, tense, and the type of conjunction used:

Basic Verb Forms

읽다 (ilkda) - to read

  • Stem: 읽 (ilk)
  • Informal: 읽어 (ilgeo)
  • Formal: 읽어요 (ilgeoyo)
  • With conjunction: 읽고 (ilkgo) - "read and..."

How Verbs Connect with Conjunctions

When combining verbs with conjunctions, the verb root changes:

  • -고 (go) connects actions: 읽고 (read and...)
  • -지만 (jiman) shows contrast: 읽지만 (read but...)
  • -어서/아서 (eoseo/aseo) shows reason: 읽어서 (because of reading...)

Additional Korean Grammar Points

Particle System

Korean uses particles to mark grammatical functions:

  • 이/가 (i/ga) - Subject marker
  • 을/를 (eul/reul) - Object marker
  • 에서 (eseo) - Location where action happens
  • 에 (e) - Destination or specific time
  • 와/과/하고 (wa/gwa/hago) - "and" (connecting nouns)

Honorific System

Korean has three main levels of politeness:

  • Informal (반말) - Used with close friends, family, younger people
  • Polite (존댓말) - Uses 요, standard respectful form
  • Formal (높임말) - Uses special honorific forms for very formal situations

Question Formation

Questions in Korean can be formed by:

  • Intonation - Rising tone at the end
  • Question words - 뭐 (what), 누구 (who), 언제 (when), 어디 (where)
  • -습니까/-ㅂ니까 - Formal question ending
